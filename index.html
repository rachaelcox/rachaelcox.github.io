I am a computational systems and synthetic biologist at Ginkgo Bioworks, where I design and analyze massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) for the DNA and RNA parts that go into vaccines, genetic therapies, and engineered organisms.

After getting a BS in Chemistry from Texas A&M in 2013, I spent several years designing and building continuous reaction systems for Eastman Chemical Company. Since then, I've decided big molecules are much more interesting than small molecules, so I went on to a PhD in Biochemistry at the University of Texas at Austin, where I studied the evolution of protein complexes in the Marcotte Lab as a Ruth L. Kirschstein NRSA Fellow. My thesis work leveraged proteomics data across the tree of life to predict the roles of human proteins in disease, with several predicted gene-disease relationships validated in frogs and mice.

I'm passionate about ways we can apply machine learning and systems biology to improve human lives, and my current work focuses on building sequence models from hundreds of thousands of measured DNA and RNA parts to learn what makes a good one. Along the way I write a lot of Python and R, and I like turning one-off analyses into tools other people can use.

Outside of the lab, I like hiking with my dog Riley, playing cards, trying new craft beers, and reading science fiction.
